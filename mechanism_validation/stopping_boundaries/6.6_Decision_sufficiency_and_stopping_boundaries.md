## 6.6 Decision sufficiency and stopping boundaries in preference clarification

The preceding analyses establish when preference ambiguity affects action selection and how clarification queries should be evaluated. We further examine the stopping mechanism of preference clarification in human–machine collaboration. Terminal states are organized into decision-sufficient stops, resource-constrained stops, and structural terminations.

Let \(R_t=\operatorname{MMR}(\Gamma_t)\), and let \(\mathcal Q_t^{\mathrm f}\) denote the set of candidate queries whose positive-probability feedback branches all admit feasible preference updates. The largest net query value is

\[
V_t^*
=
\max_{\eta\in\mathcal Q_t^{\mathrm f}}
\left\{
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{act}}(\eta)-C^{\mathrm{int}}
\right\}.
\]

The stopping states are identified by mutually exclusive boundary conditions. An action-stable decision-sufficient stop satisfies \(R_t\leq\varepsilon\). A cost-aware decision-sufficient stop satisfies \(R_t>\varepsilon\), \(\mathcal Q_t^{\mathrm f}\neq\varnothing\), and \(V_t^*\leq0\). A resource-constrained stop satisfies \(R_t>\varepsilon\), \(\mathcal Q_t^{\mathrm f}\neq\varnothing\), and \(V_t^*>0\), while either the maximum interaction round or the total interaction budget is binding. The former limits the duration of clarification, whereas the latter limits cumulative information-acquisition expenditure. Structural termination satisfies \(R_t>\varepsilon\) and \(\mathcal Q_t^{\mathrm f}=\varnothing\). It arises either because no admissible comparison query remains or because every candidate query contains an infeasible positive-probability update branch. Only the first two boundary conditions certify decision sufficiency. Resource-constrained and structural terminations limit the continuation of interaction without establishing that further information lacks decision value.

The experiment uses the 15 formal instances and the five hidden preference positions defined in Sections 6.4 and 6.5. Each trajectory starts from \([0.30,0.70]\), with \(C^{\mathrm{int}}=0.0094\), a maximum of five clarification rounds, and a total interaction budget of 0.047. Feedback branches are enumerated exactly, and terminal-state statistics are weighted by their conditional response probabilities. At each decision-sufficient stop, a one-step counterfactual selects the admissible query with the largest expected action improvement while ignoring the stopping threshold. Its response-contingent actions are then evaluated under the hidden preference. The resulting counterfactual net gain equals the expected realized-regret reduction minus the additional interaction cost.

**Table X. Stopping-state composition and counterfactual consequences**

| Stopping state | Probability (%) | Stop round | Remaining width | No. of action regions | MMR | Best net query value | Counterfactual net gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Action-stable decision sufficiency | 0.00 | – | – | – | – | – | – |
| Cost-aware decision sufficiency | 78.55 | 1.36 | 0.18442 | 3.66 | 0.02573 | −0.00495 | −0.00752 |
| Resource-constrained stop | 0.00 | – | – | – | – | – | – |
| Structural termination | 21.45 | 1.31 | 0.16031 | 2.23 | 0.00511 | – | – |

*Note: Probabilities are weighted over the exact feedback trees for 75 instance–preference scenarios. Resource-constrained stops aggregate the interaction-round and total-budget limits. Structural termination aggregates the absence of an admissible query and response-contingent update infeasibility.*

Table X shows that 78.55% of the terminal probability mass reaches decision sufficiency through the cost-aware boundary. Every cost-aware stop retains a positive-width ambiguity set and covers at least two action regions; the remaining width ranges from 0.04756 to 0.27999, and the mean number of action regions is 3.66. No action-stable stop is observed because the cost-aware boundary is reached before \(R_t\) decreases to zero. The positive MMR and multiple remaining action regions at every cost-aware stop confirm this ordering of the two decision-sufficiency boundaries. At these states, the best available query has a mean predicted net value of −0.00495. Forced continuation changes the operational action with an average probability of 25.70%, but reduces realized regret by only 0.00188 on average. After accounting for the additional interaction cost, its mean counterfactual net gain is −0.00752, and 88.51% of the cost-aware terminal probability mass has a nonpositive hidden-preference counterfactual net gain. No resource-constrained stop is observed because every trajectory reaches either a cost-aware or structural boundary within one to three rounds, before the five-round limit or total interaction budget becomes binding. The remaining 21.45% consists entirely of response-contingent update infeasibility and is therefore classified as structural termination rather than decision sufficiency. These results show that decision sufficiency can be reached while both preference and action ambiguity remain. Its relevant boundary is the marginal decision value of further information, rather than complete preference identification or action invariance alone.

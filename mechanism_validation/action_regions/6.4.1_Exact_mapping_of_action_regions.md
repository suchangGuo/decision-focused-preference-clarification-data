### 6.4.1 Exact mapping of action regions

For each feasible reconnaissance action \(x\), the normalized scalar loss is affine in the local preference weight:

\[
F(x;\lambda)
=
\lambda \widetilde T(x)
+
(1-\lambda)\widetilde L_Q(x).
\]

The optimal-value function is therefore the lower envelope of the action-specific loss lines. We identify all supported actions over \(\lambda\in[0.30,0.70]\) by recursively solving the fixed-preference problem at the interval endpoints and at the intersections of competing loss lines. Reconnaissance plans with equivalent time and information outcomes are treated as the same action class. The intersection of two adjacent lower-envelope segments defines their action-switching boundary.

All fixed-preference problems are solved to optimality by CPLEX. The resulting map is verified by resolving the problem at the midpoint of each action region and on both sides of every switching boundary. Only action classes that are optimal over a positive-length interval are retained. The exact action-region maps provide the structural basis for examining whether ambiguity sets of identical width induce comparable decision uncertainty.

Across the 15 test instances, the exact mapping identifies 80 action regions and 65 switching boundaries. Each instance contains between three and eight action regions. Among the 50 within-instance gaps between adjacent switching boundaries, 18 are smaller than the target ambiguity width of 0.04; the minimum and median gaps are 0.0085 and 0.0512, respectively. Hence, an ambiguity interval of fixed width may remain within one action region, cross a single boundary, or cover a cluster of boundaries, depending on its location. All action-region maps pass the exact verification procedure.

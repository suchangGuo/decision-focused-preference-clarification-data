### 6.4.2 Decision consequences of equal-width preference ambiguity sets

Based on the exact action-region maps obtained in Section 6.4.1, we examine whether preference ambiguity sets of identical width induce comparable decision consequences. The ambiguity width is fixed at the calibrated value (w^{\mathrm{tar}}=0.04), and the window

\[
\Gamma(c)
=
\left[c-\frac{w^{\mathrm{tar}}}{2},
c+\frac{w^{\mathrm{tar}}}{2}\right]
\]

is moved over (c\in[0.32,0.68]) in increments of 0.01. This produces 37 windows for each test instance and 555 windows across the 15 instances. A window is classified as action-stable, single-boundary, or boundary-cluster according to whether its interior contains zero, one, or at least two action-switching boundaries. For each window, the minimax-regret problem is solved to optimality by CPLEX using the exact endpoint reference losses obtained from the action-region map.

Among the 555 equal-width windows, 334 (60.18%) remain within a single action region, 192 (34.59%) cross one switching boundary, and 29 (5.23%) cover a boundary cluster. All action-stable windows admit a common optimal action and yield zero minimax regret within numerical tolerance. By contrast, the single-boundary windows have a mean minimax regret of 0.00444 and a maximum of 0.02645, whereas the boundary-cluster windows have a mean of 0.00616 and a maximum of 0.01995. Thus, holding the geometric width of the ambiguity set constant does not hold its decision consequence constant: the same width may correspond to a stable action, a change between two actions, or competition among several actions with different regret levels.

For example, for the (9\text{-}3\text{-}R) instance with seed 20261117, the windows centered at 0.40, 0.48, and 0.50 have the same width of 0.04 but cover one, two, and three action regions, respectively. Their minimax-regret values are 0, 0.00189, and 0.01231. Moreover, the largest regret among the single-boundary windows exceeds that among the boundary-cluster windows, indicating that the number of crossed boundaries alone does not determine the consequence of the remaining preference ambiguity.
